Drag the sliders. Heading 0 faces true north. Lens height 0 is the horizon, and 90 is straight overhead. Dots mark named stars, planets, the Moon, and satellites. Click a name for the catalog entry: magnitude, spectral type, distance, and where it is right now. A star with only a Hipparcos number is left off the map. The background is Axel Mellinger’s Milky Way, graded into a deep planetarium field. Hubble and JWST frames cover only a few arcminutes, so they cannot fill this view.

On a phone, this is the sky guide. A laptop has no compass, and its camera is not pointed at the sky, so the sliders are how you look around here.

The photograph, the constellation lines, and the names turn as you turn the phone. North comes from the compass, corrected to true north. Tip the phone flat once if north feels off, or wave it in a figure-eight. The browser asks to use motion sensors, then the rear camera. If you decline the camera, the photograph keeps turning.
